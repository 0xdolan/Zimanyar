## What is Kurmanji?

Kurdish is an **Indo-European** language in its own right: a **macrolanguage** made up of closely related varieties, with its own history, literature and standard written forms. As an Indo-European language it is a distant relative of most languages of Europe and South Asia, English included, and it shares some vocabulary with its neighbours, but it is a distinct language. **Northern Kurdish**, called **Kurmanji** (*Kurmancî*), is the largest: roughly 15 to 20 million speakers in

* **Bakur**, Northern Kurdistan, in Turkey (Amed / Diyarbakır, Wan, Mêrdîn, Colemêrg …),
* **Rojava**, Western Kurdistan, in Syria (Qamişlo, Kobanî, Efrîn),
* **Behdînan**, the Duhok region of Başûr (where the local form is called *Badînî*),
* the north of **Rojhilat**, Eastern Kurdistan (in Iran), and **Armenia** and **Georgia**,
* and a large diaspora in Europe.

Its closest relative is **Central Kurdish (Sorani)**, which has its own course in this series.

## The writing system in one minute

Kurmanji is written in the **Hawar alphabet**, a Latin alphabet of 31 letters designed in 1932 by Celadet Bedirxan. It is almost perfectly **phonetic**: each letter is one sound and each sound is one letter. Once you know the letters you can pronounce any word you read.

In Başûr and Rojhilat Kurmanji is also written in the Arabic-based Kurdish alphabet; every word in this course is shown in that script too.

## What makes Kurmanji grammar interesting

| idea | example | lesson |
|---|---|---|
| every noun is **masculine or feminine** | *bav m* father, *jin f* woman | 7 |
| nouns have two **cases**, direct and oblique | *jin* → *jinê* | 9 |
| a linking ending (**ezafe**) joins nouns to adjectives and owners | *mala min* my house | 8 |
| the verb comes **last** | *Ez nan dixwim* · I bread eat | 10 |
| in the past, transitive verbs are **ergative**: the doer is oblique and the verb agrees with the object | *Min tu dîtî* I saw you | 14 |
