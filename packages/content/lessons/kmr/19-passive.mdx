## hatin + infinitive

Kurmanji forms the passive with the verb **hatin** *to come*, conjugated in any tense, followed by the **infinitive** of the main verb: literally *the bread came eating* = *the bread was eaten*.

| tense | form of hatin | example | meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| present | **tê** | nan **tê** xwarin | bread is (being) eaten |
| past | **hat** | nan **hat** xwarin | the bread was eaten |
| present perfect | **hatiye** | nan **hatiye** xwarin | the bread has been eaten |
| future | **dê bê** | nan **dê bê** xwarin | the bread will be eaten |
| subjunctive | **bê** | divê nan **bê** xwarin | the bread must be eaten |

Because *hatin* is intransitive, the passive is **never ergative**: the patient is direct and *hatin* agrees with it: *ez hatim girtin* “I was arrested”, *ew hatin girtin* “they were arrested”.

## The state: participle + copula

For a resulting **state**, use the past participle (past stem + **-î**) with the copula: *derî vekirî ye* “the door is open”. Compare:

| event (passive) | state |
|---|---|
| derî **tê vekirin** *the door is being opened* | derî **vekirî ye** *the door is open* |

## The agent

The doer is usually left out. It can be expressed with **ji aliyê … ve** *from the side of*: *ji aliyê hikûmetê ve hat ragihandin* “it was announced by the government”.

## Summary

* Passive = **hatin** (any tense) + **infinitive**: tê xwarin, hat xwarin, hatiye xwarin.
* Not ergative: the patient is direct, hatin agrees with it.
* State: participle + copula (vekirî ye).
