## Words and spaces

Kurmanji is written left to right with spaces between words, like English. Unlike Sorani, most grammatical pieces are **separate words**: prepositions (*li malê* “at home”), the copula (*ez kurd im* “I am Kurdish”) and the future particle (*ez ê biçim*). Only real suffixes (case, ezafe, *-ek*, plural *-an*) and verb prefixes (*di-, bi-, na-*) are attached.

| written apart | attached |
|---|---|
| ez kurd **im** *I am Kurdish* | **di**xwînim *I read* |
| **li** malê *at home* | mal**ê** *house (oblique)* |
| **ji** min **re** *for me* | pirtûk**ek** *a book* |

## Capital letters and apostrophes

Capitals start sentences and names. When a case or ezafe ending is added to a name, especially a foreign one or an abbreviation, an **apostrophe** separates it: *Amedê* but *HDP'ê*. Native Kurdish names usually take the ending directly: *Rojdayê*.

## Punctuation

The same as English: . , ; : ? ! « » ( ). Quotation marks are often « ».

## Kurmanji in Arabic script

In Behdînan (Duhok) and in Rojhilat, Kurmanji is also written in the Kurdish Arabic alphabet of the Sorani course. zimankit transliterates automatically. Note how short *i* disappears, because that script does not write it:

<Sentences lang="kmr" items={[["Ez kurd im.","I am Kurdish."],["Navê min Rojda ye.","My name is Rojda."],["Em li Amedê dijîn.","We live in Diyarbakır."]]} />

## Summary

* Spaces separate prepositions, the copula and particles; only suffixes and verb prefixes attach.
* Names + endings often take an apostrophe: HDP'ê.
* Numbers: yek du sê çar pênc şeş heft heşt neh deh.
