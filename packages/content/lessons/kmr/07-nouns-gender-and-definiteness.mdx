## Masculine and feminine

Every Kurmanji noun is **masculine (m)** or **feminine (f)**. For people it follows sex (*bav* father m, *jin* woman f); for everything else you must learn it with the word, as in German or French. The gender does not change the noun itself, but it chooses the endings of lessons 8 and 9, so **always learn nouns with their gender**:

<Words lang="kmr" words="bav,bira,mirov,jin,keç,mal,bajar,pirtûk,ziman,welat" />

| m | f |
|---|---|
| bav *father*, bira *brother*, kur *son*, mirov *person* | jin *woman*, keç *girl*, dê *mother*, xwişk *sister* |
| bajar *city*, gund *village*, welat *country*, ziman *language*, nan *bread*, xanî *house (building)* | mal *home*, pirtûk *book*, kitêb *book*, av *water*, roj *day*, şev *night*, sêv *apple* |

## “The”: nothing

A bare noun is **definite** or generic: *jin* “the woman” or “woman”. There is no word for *the*.

## “A”: -ek

Add **-ek** (after a vowel **-yek**): *jinek* “a woman”, *çiyayek* “a mountain”, *xaniyek* “a house”. For *some* (plural) use **hin**: *hin pirtûk* “some books”.

## Plural: often nothing!

In the **direct** case the plural is **unmarked**: *keç* = “the girl” or “the girls”. The verb and any endings show the number:

<Sentences lang="kmr" ipa items={[["Keç hat.","The girl came."],["Keç hatin.","The girls came."],["Jinek hat.","A woman came."],["Hin mirov hatin.","Some people came."]]} />

In the oblique case (lesson 9) and with ezafe (lesson 8) the plural **is** marked: *-an*, *-ên*.

## Practice

1. *a book*, *a mountain*, *a house* (xanî).
2. What is the gender of *mal*, *bajar*, *jin*?

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. pirtûkek, çiyayek, xaniyek.
2. *mal* f, *bajar* m, *jin* f.

</details>

## Summary

* Every noun is m or f: learn the gender with the noun.
* *the* = bare noun; *a* = **-ek / -yek**; *some* = **hin**.
* The direct plural is unmarked; the verb shows the number.
