## Present perfect

> past stem + **-ime, -iyî, -iye, -ine** (after a vowel: **-me, -yî, -ye, -ne**)

The endings are the copula fused to the past stem. A stem-final *î* shortens to *i* before *y* (*nivîsî → nivîsiye*).

* **Intransitive**: the subject is direct and the verb agrees with it: *ez hatime* “I have come”.
* **Transitive**: ergative, like every past-stem tense: *min ew dîtiye* “I have seen him”, with the verb agreeing with the object.

## Past perfect

> past stem + **-ibû** (after a vowel **-bû**) + past ending

*ez hatibûm* “I had come”, *min ew dîtibû* “I had seen him”.

## Meaning

As in English, for past events with present relevance. The present perfect is also used for things you know only from hearsay: *ew çûye Ewropayê* “he has (reportedly) gone to Europe”.

## Summary

* Present perfect: past stem + **-ime -iyî -iye -ine** (hatime, dîtiye).
* Past perfect: past stem + **-ibû** + ending (hatibûm).
* Transitive perfects are ergative: *min … dîtiye*.
