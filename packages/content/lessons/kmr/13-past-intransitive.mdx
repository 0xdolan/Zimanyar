## Transitive or intransitive?

From now on you must know whether a verb is **transitive** (it takes a direct object: *eat something*, *see someone*) or **intransitive** (it does not: *come, go, fall, sleep*). Kurmanji builds the past of the two kinds in completely different ways. This lesson covers the easy kind, intransitive verbs. The [verb reference](../verbs/) marks every verb.

## The simple past

> **past stem** + personal ending (the subject is in the **direct** case)

| person | after a consonant | after a vowel |
|---|---|---|
| 1sg | **-im** | **-m** |
| 2sg | **-î** | **-yî** |
| 3sg | · | · |
| pl | **-in** | **-n** |

The negative is **ne-**: *nehatim* “I didn't come”.

## In sentences

<Sentences lang="kmr" ipa items={[["Ez duh hatim.","I came yesterday."],["Ew çûn bazarê.","They went to the market."],["Zarok ket.","The child fell."],["Tu çima nehatî?","Why didn't you come?"],["Em li malê man.","We stayed at home."]]} />

The subject is direct (*ez, tu, ew*) and the verb agrees with it, just as in English. In the next lesson this changes.

## Practice

Put into the past: *ez diçim*, *ew tê*, *em dimînin*.

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

*ez çûm*, *ew hat*, *em man*.

</details>

## Summary

* Intransitive past = past stem + **-im -î, -in**; negative **ne-**.
* Subject in the direct case; the verb agrees with it.
